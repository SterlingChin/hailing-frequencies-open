  1. Shows connection status only. Mute, microphone and hang-up stay in the Twilio Dev Phone window placed beside the console. With an embedded dialer later, those controls join this pill.
  2. Reads “Demo profile loaded”, not “verified”. The profile, case and each task are separate records, so they outlast this screen.

Emphasis. A fixed three-zone workspace: who (crew), what is being said (voice), who is working (specialists). A viewer can find each zone by color, even in a small video player.
Voice support. The current line is the largest text on screen, and the speaker bar shows who has the floor. Specialist work is kept in the rail so it does not compete with the conversation.
Tradeoff. The densest of the three. It reads best on a large desktop and needs real work to adapt to a phone. The elbow framing can also start to look like decoration rather than structure.

  1. One expressive element. A slow breath means the agent is speaking; an outward ring means it is listening. A text label always sits beneath it. With reduced motion, it holds still.
  2. Compact context strip. A corrected symptom replaces the old one in place with an “Updated” tag, so the change shows without opening anything.

Emphasis. The feeling of a single agent that stays with you. Captions are large enough to follow with the sound off, and there is little else on screen.
Voice support. Clearest of the three for turn-taking and interruptions: the listening and speaking change is the biggest signal on screen. It adapts to a 390px phone almost directly.
Tradeoff. Specialist work reads as secondary. On camera, Engineering’s hand-off to Logistics is easy to miss, and longer case history sits one step away.

Annotated flow

The same steps in every concept. Solid cards are the smallest complete demo; dashed cards are stretch features to add on top.

01 · CORE · START
Contact
Product name, ship and equipment context, any open case, and a single “Call support” action showing the number to dial from Dev Phone.
02–03 · CORE · CONNECTING
Greeting and profile
Status goes Connecting → Connected. The tagline appears as a caption. After the spoken phrase: “Demo profile loaded”, then equipment and preferences fill in.
04–05 · CORE · ACTIVE CALL
Diagnosis
The symptom is added to case HF-042 and an Engineering task opens with its assignment. The caller can keep talking or correct the symptom.
06 · CORE · RESULT
Specialist result
Finding, source from the catalog, and one proposed next step. Actions: try the step, ask a question, or bring in another specialist.
07 · STRETCH · PARTS
Logistics
A visible hand-off from Engineering. One compatible regulator at the station depot, with “no reservation made” stated plainly.
08 · CORE · FOLLOW-UP
Requested text
Message preview, channel, destination label and reason. Delivery goes Requested → Queued → Delivered, or “Delivery not confirmed”.
09 · CORE · AFTER THE CALL
Case recap
Diagnosis, steps tried, open items, messages sent. “Your call has ended. The parts check is still pending.” Action: Resume case.
STRETCH
Later layers
SMS reply continues the case (asks which case if several match) · photo upload attached to the case · requested callback · Klingon support · Borg emergency.
Reconnecting. Status pill changes to “Reconnecting”, captions pause and grey out, and the case stays put. Once recovery fails, a “Call again” action appears.
Specialist delayed. The task card reads “Still working, longer than usual”, and the rest of the screen stays usable.
Specialist failed. “Engineering couldn’t finish this check.” Offers Retry or Ask Logistics. No diagnosis is shown.
Symptom changed. The new symptom replaces the old one, and any earlier result is marked as replaced (see 1b).
Delivery failed. The result stays in the case. Another eligible channel is offered along with “Try again”.
Late result. A result that arrives after hang-up attaches to its own task and case, and appears in the recap marked “arrived after call”.

Type, color, shape and motion

1A BRIDGE CONSOLE
ANTONIO LABELS
Atkinson Hyperlegible for captions and body, chosen for legibility at small video sizes.
Amber: structure and Engineering · Coral: agent voice · Lavender: caller and Logistics · Green: connection and “ready”.
Shape: 8px inner corners, 28–56px elbows. 8px spacing grid, 16px gutters.
Motion: speaker bar color swaps in 150ms; a new result card slides 12px in from the rail. No looping animation.
1B PERSONAL COMMUNICATOR
Bricolage Grotesque
One family throughout. Light weight at 48px for the live caption, semibold for labels.
Mint: the agent and listening · Gold: Engineering · Periwinkle: Logistics · Red: end call only.
Shape: pills and 22px cards. Generous spacing, 40px margins.
Motion: a 3.2s breath while speaking, an outward ring while listening. Reduced motion holds a static ring.
1C COMMUNICATIONS DESK
IBM Plex + Mono
Sans for content, Mono for timestamps and record labels.
Ink: voice · Ochre: Engineering · Blue: Logistics · Green: SMS and confirmed states.
Shape: 10px cards, a circle for voice, a square for tasks. 64px time column.
Motion: new entries fade up 8px. Delivery steps fill only when the record changes.
Shared components: connection pill · speaker label (text + glyph + color) · caption line (live and earlier) · specialist task card (Queued, Working, Delayed, Result ready, Failed, Replaced) · next-step card (Proposed / Confirmed by caller) · follow-up card with delivery steps · context strip · preference row with “Change”.

Use 1a as the desktop recording screen. It does the best job of showing a viewer who is being helped, which specialist is working, and what changed, and its framing carries the humor without needing more copy.

Borrow two things. Put 1b’s caption treatment and listening/speaking element in 1a’s center panel, and use 1b as the 390px mobile companion. Use 1c’s timeline for the Case recap and the text follow-up screens. All three can share the task card, delivery steps and speaker labels, so they will still feel like one product.

Avoid using 1c’s timeline during the live call in 1a. Two scrolling regions would compete for attention on camera.
